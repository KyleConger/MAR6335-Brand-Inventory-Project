## Brand Image–Brand Identity Gap Measurement

A key component of the Customer-Based Brand Equity Model is **Brand Associations**, the constellation of concepts that are related to the brand in the mind of the consumer. This is sometimes referred to as brand meaning or brand image.

It is important to note that brand image is influenced and shaped by, not just the brand’s messaging, but also (in Doug Holt’s terms) the “stories” that other entities (e.g., pop culture, influencers, word-of-mouth) tell about the brand. As a consequence, the resultant brand image is not entirely under the brand’s control.

Therefore, we draw an important distinction between:

- **Brand identity** — the brand’s intended set of associations (what the brand would like consumers to associate with the brand)
- **Brand image** — the actual brand associations salient to consumers (how they actually perceive the brand)

Commonly, some degree of disconnect exists between brand identity and brand image, sometimes to the detriment of brand equity. Part of what brand management hopes to accomplish with a brand audit is to identify any identity-image disconnects, or gaps, as well as potential remedies.

A “gap” analysis of brand identity and brand image begins with the Brand Inventory. In addition to documenting the brand’s heritage, positioning, and go-to-market strategy (4 P’s), the brand inventory should incorporate the brand identity analysis below.

Advertising is often a very useful source for this analysis, but other brand elements can offer insights as well, e.g., brand name, logo, trade characters (like Flo from Progressive Insurance), slogans, packaging, etc.

**Note:** The brand image measurement required to complete the gap analysis will occur as part of the Brand Exploratory.

--- SLIDE 4 ---
Laddering Technique - Means-End Structures
Assumptions:
Consumers buy products primarily to do something, i.e., solve important consumption problems
Product knowledge is organized in terms of what products do -- their consequences.
Consequences vary in abstraction
Attributes, consequences, and values are linked to form an associative network of brand perceptions -- a means-end chain.

--- SLIDE 6 ---
Laddering Technique - Means-end Structures (3)
Concrete attributes 
Cognitive representation of physical characteristics
Can be perceived directly
Example: price
Abstract attributes
Abstract representation or “chunk” standing for several more concrete attributes
Not directly measurable
Example: good quality 

--- SLIDE 7 ---
Laddering Technique - Means-end Structures (4)
Functional consequences
Whether the product does what it is supposed to do
Whether it functions appropriately
Example: Handles easily
Psychosocial consequences
How product makes person feel 
How that person perceives that others feel about them as a result of using the brand
Example: Others see me as special

--- SLIDE 8 ---
Laddering Technique - Means-End Structures (5)
Instrumental values
Preferred modes of conduct
Abstract consequences of using it
Example: Being center of attention 
Terminal values
Preferred end states
Very abstract consequences
Example: Security

--- SLIDE 9 ---
Laddering Questioning Technique
Keep asking “Why?”
Find a hierarchical structure of attributes, consequences and values that consumers have about the brand.

--- SLIDE 10 ---
Example: Bud Light Ladder 1
   Why do you drink Bud Light?
Because it is healthier and less filling. (functional consequences) 
Why is it important for you to be healthy? 
Because I can accomplish a lot after drinking a Bud Light.I can even accomplish more! (functional consequence) 
Why is it important for you to accomplish more?
Because I feel better about myself when I accomplish a lot. (psychosocial consequence) 

--- SLIDE 11 ---
Example: Bud Light Ladder 2
Why do you drink Bud Light?
I drink it because I watch the calories. (physical attribute)
Why is it important for you to watch the calories? 
Because I want to avoid gaining weight! (functional consequence)
Why do you want to avoid gaining weight?
I want to stay skinny. That’s the personal image I have about myself. It is also what other people expect me to be. (psychosocial consequence) 

--- SLIDE 12 ---
Example: Bud Light Ladder 2 (Continued)
Why do you want to meet people’s expectations?
I feel they will only accept me when I am like this. (instrumental value)
Why is it important for you that people accept you?
Because I feel much better about myself when I know that other people think I look good. (terminal value)

Conclusion
Laddering is an excellent technique for digging  deeper into consumers’ brand preferences.
